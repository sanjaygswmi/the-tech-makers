## Brief 1 — Technical SEO Checklist Before Launching or Redesigning a Website

- **Primary service:** Digital Marketing & SEO (`digital-marketing-seo`)
- **Secondary service:** Web Development (`web-development`), only where implementation details are covered
- **Suggested category:** SEO
- **Audience:** Business owners, marketing leads, and teams planning a website launch or redesign
- **Intent:** Practical checklist to reduce preventable indexing, crawlability, analytics, and migration issues
- **Proposed meta title:** Technical SEO Checklist for Website Launches
- **Proposed meta description:** Use this technical SEO checklist before a website launch or redesign to review crawlability, indexing, redirects, metadata, performance, analytics, and post-launch monitoring.
- **Primary CTA:** Request a website SEO or launch-readiness review

### Outline
1. Why technical SEO belongs in launch planning—not only after launch
2. Crawlability and indexability
   - Robots directives and XML sitemap
   - Canonicals and index/noindex rules
   - Internal links and orphaned pages
3. URL changes and migration safety
   - URL inventory and redirect map
   - Avoid redirect chains and broken internal links
4. Page-level essentials
   - Titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text where meaningful
   - Structured data validation when applicable
5. Performance and mobile usability
   - Core Web Vitals as diagnostic signals, not a guarantee of ranking
   - Responsive layouts and real-device checks
6. Measurement and launch monitoring
   - Analytics and conversion events
   - Search Console verification, crawl checks, and 404 monitoring
7. A reusable pre-launch checklist
8. What to monitor in the first days and weeks after launch

### Evidence and quality requirements
- Include a downloadable/copyable checklist only after the team validates it against its actual launch process.
- Distinguish required technical checks from optional recommendations.
- Avoid promising that completing the checklist guarantees rankings or traffic.
- Add a practical migration example if a real, anonymized example is available.

### Internal links
- Primary: `/services/digital-marketing-seo`
- Secondary: `/services/web-development`
- Optional: one relevant existing article and the contact/enquiry page

## Brief 2 — How to Choose the Right Website Architecture for a Growing Business

- **Primary service:** Web Development (`web-development`)
- **Secondary service:** Technology Consulting (`technology-consulting`), if discussing discovery and technical roadmap decisions
- **Suggested category:** Web Design & Development
- **Audience:** Business owners and product/operations leaders planning a new website, portal, or platform rebuild
- **Intent:** Help readers compare architecture options and ask better questions before committing to a build
- **Proposed meta title:** How to Choose Website Architecture for Your Business
- **Proposed meta description:** Compare common website architecture choices by business needs, integrations, content workflows, scalability, maintenance, and total cost of ownership.
- **Primary CTA:** Discuss requirements and architecture options with the development team

### Outline
1. Start with business requirements, not a technology trend
2. Separate the website’s content needs from application workflows
3. Common approaches and when they fit
   - CMS-led marketing website
   - Custom web application
   - E-commerce platform
   - Headless or API-driven architecture
4. Questions about integrations, data ownership, roles, and approval workflows
5. Scalability versus overengineering
6. Security, maintenance, hosting, and operational ownership
7. How to evaluate build cost beyond the initial quote
8. A decision checklist and questions to ask a prospective partner

### Evidence and quality requirements
- Present options neutrally; do not claim one stack is best for every business.
- Use a simple decision matrix based on requirements, not unsupported performance claims.
- Clearly separate examples from recommendations for a specific client.

### Internal links
- Primary: `/services/web-development`
- Secondary: `/services/technology-consulting` when the article covers roadmap/discovery

## Brief 3 — Where AI Automation Helps a Business—and Where It Does Not

- **Primary service:** AI Solutions (`ai-solutions`)
- **Secondary service:** AI Chatbot Development (`ai-chatbot-development`) only in the conversational-AI section
- **Suggested category:** Artificial Intelligence (create only if the existing category structure cannot represent this topic)
- **Audience:** Business leaders and operations teams exploring practical AI automation
- **Intent:** Qualify use cases and set realistic expectations before investing
- **Proposed meta title:** AI Automation for Business: Use Cases and Limits
- **Proposed meta description:** Learn where AI automation can help business workflows, where human review is essential, and how to evaluate data readiness, risks, integrations, and ROI.
- **Primary CTA:** Discuss a specific workflow for an AI automation assessment

### Outline
1. Start with the workflow problem, not the model
2. Strong candidates for automation
   - Repetitive document processing and classification
   - Knowledge retrieval with approved sources
   - Drafting and summarization with review
   - Routing and triage
3. Cases needing human judgment or stronger controls
   - High-impact decisions
   - Ambiguous, sensitive, or poorly documented processes
   - Workflows where errors are expensive or hard to reverse
4. Data quality, access permissions, privacy, and retention
5. Integration and exception handling
6. How to pilot safely
   - Baseline the current workflow
   - Define acceptance criteria and escalation
   - Test representative edge cases
   - Measure quality, time saved, and operating cost
7. Questions to answer before scaling
8. A practical readiness checklist

### Evidence and quality requirements
- Avoid claiming guaranteed cost savings, accuracy, or fully autonomous performance.
- Explain that the right design may include human review, fallbacks, and auditability.
- Do not include sensitive client examples without permission.

### Internal links
- Primary: `/services/ai-solutions`
- Secondary: `/services/ai-chatbot-development` only where conversational interfaces are relevant
- Optional: `/services/technology-consulting` for discovery and prioritization

## Brief 4 — Website vs Mobile App: How to Decide What Your Business Actually Needs

- **Primary service:** App Development (`app-development`)
- **Secondary service:** Web Development (`web-development`)
- **Suggested category:** App Development (create only if useful; otherwise use the closest existing category consistently)
- **Audience:** Business owners evaluating a customer-facing digital product
- **Intent:** Compare reach, user experience, device capabilities, maintenance, distribution, and total cost
- **Proposed meta title:** Website vs Mobile App: Which Does Your Business Need?
- **Proposed meta description:** Compare a responsive website, progressive web app, and native mobile app to choose the right option for your users, workflows, budget, and long-term maintenance.
- **Primary CTA:** Discuss product requirements and platform options

### Outline
1. Clarify the user problem and frequency of use
2. Responsive website: reach, discoverability, and low-friction access
3. Progressive web app: when app-like behavior may be enough
4. Native or cross-platform mobile app: device capabilities and repeated use
5. Compare:
   - Installation and distribution
   - Offline behavior and push notifications
   - Hardware/API access
   - SEO and acquisition
   - Release cycles and maintenance
   - Analytics and support
6. When a website-first approach makes sense
7. When a mobile app is justified
8. When a shared backend and multiple front ends may be appropriate
9. A decision matrix and discovery questions

### Evidence and quality requirements
- Explain trade-offs without pushing an app where a responsive website would suffice.
- Avoid fixed cost estimates unless grounded in a defined scope and current pricing.
- Distinguish platform choice from product-market validation.

### Internal links
- Primary: `/services/app-development`
- Secondary: `/services/web-development`
- Optional: `/services/product-development` if validation and MVP scope are covered
